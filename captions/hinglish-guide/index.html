Hinglish captions: writing Hindi in English letters

Why Hinglish captions work, how people spell common Hindi words in English letters, and tips to keep a Hinglish caption clear to readers from different states.

Hinglish is not a language with rules. It is how millions of people type: Hindi words in the English alphabet, mixed with English words, the way the two are spoken. For a caption it has one big advantage: it needs no special keyboard and it looks casual and friendly.

How people spell it

There is no standard spelling, so you will see "yaar", "yar" and "yaaar" for the same word. A few habits are common. Long vowels are often written double (aa, ee, oo). The sound of "ha" at the end is written "ha" or "h". "Hai" and "hain" are the most common words, and "nahi" is usually written without the final "n".

Tips for a clear caption

  • Keep it short. Long lines in Roman Hindi are harder to read than long lines in Devanagari.
  • Be consistent. Choose one spelling for each word within a post.
  • Mind your readers. Someone from another state may follow your Hindi but not slang from your town.
  • Mix with purpose. A line that is half English, half Hindi should feel natural, not forced.

Hinglish or Devanagari?

Hinglish is quicker and feels informal. Devanagari looks neat and respectful, and it is better when the post is about a festival, a family or a serious topic. Pick by mood. We have lists in both forms: Hinglish captions, Hindi captions and Hindi lines about friends.

Questions people ask

Is Hinglish correct Hindi?

It is not a formal language. It is a way of writing everyday speech. For official letters and school work use proper Hindi in Devanagari; for chat and captions Hinglish is fine.
