Captions for a business or shop post

How a small shop, tutor, home baker or freelancer can write captions that sell without sounding like an advert: what to say, what to leave out, with samples.

A business caption has a different job from a personal one. It must still sound like a person, but it also needs to say what you offer, to whom, and what the reader should do next. The best small-business captions are plain, specific and polite.

What every business caption needs

  1. What it is. "Fresh gulab jamun, made today" is better than "something sweet".
  2. Who it is for or when. "Orders for Sunday close on Friday night."
  3. One next step. Message us, visit us, call us. Not three of them.

Four sample shapes

New product: "New this week: hand-block printed cotton kurtas in three colours. Sizes S to XXL. Message us to order."
Offer: "Diwali box of dry fruits, packed on request. Orders open till the 28th."
Behind the scenes: "Six in the morning and the first batch is out of the oven. Thank you to everyone who ordered."
Customer thank-you: "Thank you to Priya for the lovely photo with her cake. We enjoy making these."

Always ask permission before you show a customer's photo or name.

Leave out

  • Promises you cannot keep (best in town, guaranteed results).
  • Prices that change often, unless you update the post.
  • Personal phone numbers of staff or family.
  • Medical or legal claims. These break advertising rules in India.

Keeping it honest

If you are paid to post about a product, the Advertising Standards Council of India expects you to say so clearly. We do not give legal advice; read the council's guidelines on influencer advertising before you start. For ready messages to answer customers, see the WhatsApp message templates.

Questions people ask

How often should a small business post?

Regular is better than frequent. Three useful posts a week that you can keep up are better than a daily post you stop after a month.
