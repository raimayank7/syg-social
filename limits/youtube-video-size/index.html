YouTube video resolution and aspect ratio

YouTube's help page on resolution: 16:9 standard, the list from 240p to 8K, and what happens with vertical videos and black bars.

YouTube Help says that the standard aspect ratio for YouTube on a computer is 16:9, and that the player adapts to the size of each video. For the default 16:9 ratio it lists these sizes to encode at:

NameSize
4320p (8K)7680 x 4320
2160p (4K)3840 x 2160
1440p (2K)2560 x 1440
1080p (HD)1920 x 1080
720p (HD)1280 x 720
480p (SD)854 x 480
360p (SD)640 x 360
240p (SD)426 x 240

The page adds that YouTube may add padding around vertical (9:16) videos on computer browsers, and advises you not to add black bars or padding to your own video, because it stops the player fitting the video. It also notes that since 2022 YouTube has been removing support for playback between 4K and 8K, for example 5K.

Read on 14 November 2026 at support.google.com/youtube/answer/6375112. Apps can change a limit without notice; check the app before an important post.

Which to use

Most phone videos are 1080p and that is a good default. Keep your video in its natural shape: do not add bars to turn a vertical video into a wide one.

Questions people ask

Which resolution should I use?

Most phone videos are 1080p, which is on the list. Keep the original shape of your video.

Should I add black bars to a vertical video?

YouTube advises against adding padding or bars to your video, because it stops the player fitting the video.
