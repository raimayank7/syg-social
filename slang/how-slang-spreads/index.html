How slang spreads and why it changes so fast

Where new slang words come from, why some stay and most disappear, and what that means when you try to use a word you only just learned.

Every generation makes words to talk about its own life. Some of those words come from songs and films, some from gaming and short videos, some from a joke inside one group of friends that then escapes. What makes a word spread is simple: it says something people wanted to say and had no short way to say.

Where new words come from

  • Music and films. A line from a song becomes a phrase friends use for something else.
  • Gaming and streaming. Words typed in a hurry during a match become everyday chat words.
  • Short videos. A sound or caption repeated by millions turns into a word within days.
  • Languages mixing. In India, Hindi and English trade words all the time. Timepass, jugaad and scene are everyday English-chat words now.
  • Shortening. People shorten long phrases because typing is slow: "to be honest" becomes "tbh".

Why most words disappear

A slang word is a signal that you belong to a group. Once everyone, including people outside the group, uses it, the signal weakens, and the original group moves to a new word. That is why a word can feel fresh for a year and old the next. Words that fill a real gap, such as "ghosting", tend to last. Words that are only fashionable, such as a particular compliment, fade.

Using a new word safely

  1. See it used a few times before you try it.
  2. Check the meaning on a page like our Gen Z slang list, because the obvious guess is sometimes wrong.
  3. Use it in a place where a mistake does not matter.
  4. If it sounds forced when you say it aloud, drop it.

You do not need to keep up with every word. Most people use a handful of slang words their whole life and do fine. If you want a quick reference, the A to Z list gathers every word on this site.

Questions people ask

Is slang bad English?

No. Slang is informal English. It is fine with friends and wrong for an official letter. The skill is knowing which setting you are in.
