Internet culture terms

Words about memes, trends and online behaviour.

The internet has produced words for things people do online that did not exist before. This page explains the most common ones without jargon, so you can follow a conversation or talk to a teenager without a dictionary.

A term can be harmless in one group and hurtful in another. Think before using words that describe other people.

  • Meme

    An image, video or phrase that spreads and is copied with changes.

    Example: "that meme is everywhere"

  • Viral

    Spreading fast across many people.

    Example: "the video went viral"

  • Troll

    A person who posts to annoy others.

    Example: "do not feed the troll"

  • Doomscrolling

    Reading bad news for a long time.

    Example: "I lost an hour doomscrolling"

  • Cringe

    Something that makes you feel embarrassed.

    Example: "that ad was cringe"

  • Lurk

    To read a group without posting.

    Example: "I mostly lurk in the group"

  • Flame war

    An angry online argument.

    Example: "the thread became a flame war"

  • Clapback

    A sharp reply to criticism.

    Example: "her clapback was calm and clear"

  • Ratio

    When replies to a post outnumber its likes, showing people disagree.

    Example: "the post got ratioed"

  • Spam

    Unwanted repeated messages.

    Example: "the group is full of spam"

  • Hashtag

    A word with # that groups posts on a topic.

    Example: "use a hashtag for the event"

  • Handle

    Your username on an app.

    Example: "what is your handle?"

  • Algorithm

    The rules an app uses to decide what you see.

    Example: "the algorithm showed me this"

  • Influencer

    A person who has a following and works with brands.

    Example: "she is a food influencer"

  • Reels

    Short vertical videos on some apps.

    Example: "I made a reel"

  • Story

    A post that disappears after a day.

    Example: "it is in my story"

  • Trending

    Being talked about by many people right now.

    Example: "it is trending"

  • Clickbait

    A title made to make you click, often misleading.

    Example: "the title was clickbait"

  • Fake news

    False information presented as news.

    Example: "do not forward fake news"

  • Troll farm

    A group paid to post and push opinions.

    Example: "it looked like a troll farm"

Questions people ask

Do these words mean the same everywhere?

Mostly, but not always. Slang changes with the group, the app and the year. Use the meaning here as the usual one and check with the context.

Why is a word not listed?

We list words that are common and not mainly abusive. Tell us on the contact page if you want one added.
