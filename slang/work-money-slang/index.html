Work and money slang

Terms from offices, start-ups and side businesses.

The way we work has produced its own words: some from the office, some from start-ups and freelancing. These are the ones you will hear in meetings, job posts and videos about careers.

Some of these words are fashionable and may not last. Use the plain version in a job application.

  • Side hustle

    Extra work done for money alongside a main job.

    Example: "her side hustle is baking"

  • Quiet quitting

    Doing only what the job requires, nothing extra.

    Example: "he is quiet quitting"

  • WFH

    Work from home.

    Example: "I am wfh on Fridays"

  • Burnout

    Exhaustion from long stress at work.

    Example: "she took a break after burnout"

  • Gig

    A short job or project, often freelance.

    Example: "I got a design gig"

  • Ping

    To send a quick message.

    Example: "ping me when you are free"

  • Circle back

    Return to a topic later.

    Example: "let us circle back next week"

  • Bandwidth

    How much work a person can take on.

    Example: "I have no bandwidth this week"

  • Deadline

    The time by which work must be done.

    Example: "the deadline is Monday"

  • KPI

    Key performance indicator; a number that measures how well you are doing.

    Example: "our kpi is sales"

  • OOO

    Out of office.

    Example: "I am ooo till Monday"

  • EOD

    End of day.

    Example: "send it by eod"

  • Pitch

    A short presentation to win work or money.

    Example: "I have a pitch tomorrow"

  • Freelance

    Working for different clients without one employer.

    Example: "she works freelance"

  • Flex

    To show off, often money or things.

    Example: "he likes to flex his car"

  • Broke

    Having no money for now.

    Example: "I am broke till the salary"

  • Passive income

    Money that comes without daily work, after setup.

    Example: "rent is passive income"

  • Upskill

    Learn new skills for work.

    Example: "I am upskilling in Excel"

  • Networking

    Meeting people to find opportunities.

    Example: "networking helped me find work"

  • Onboarding

    The process of a new person joining a team.

    Example: "onboarding takes a week"

Questions people ask

Do these words mean the same everywhere?

Mostly, but not always. Slang changes with the group, the app and the year. Use the meaning here as the usual one and check with the context.

Why is a word not listed?

We list words that are common and not mainly abusive. Tell us on the contact page if you want one added.
