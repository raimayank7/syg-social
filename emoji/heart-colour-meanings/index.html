Heart emoji colours and what they usually mean

What each coloured heart emoji is commonly used for, why red is not always romantic, and how to pick a heart for friends, family and work.

There is no official meaning for each colour of heart. The meanings below are how many people use them, and they differ by group, country and age. Use them as a guide, not a rule.

HeartCommon use
❤️ redLove, affection, strong thanks. For a partner, but also used for family and friends.
🧡 orangeWarm friendship, energy; often used for festivals and sunsets.
💛 yellowFriendship, happiness, sunshine; usually not romantic.
💚 greenNature, growth, health, luck; also used by sports fans and for eco themes.
💙 blueCalm, trust, loyalty; also a team colour.
💜 purpleCare, creativity, support; used by fans of music acts and for kindness.
🤍 whitePure, peaceful, remembrance; sometimes used in weddings and condolence messages.
🖤 blackDark humour, grief or a stylish, cool tone, depending on context.
🤎 brownWarmth and comfort, often for food, coffee and autumn.
💔 brokenHeartbreak or sadness.
🩷 pinkSoft affection, tenderness, sweetness.
🩵 light blueGentle calm and friendship.

Choosing a heart

  • To friends and family: yellow, orange and purple feel warm without being romantic.
  • To a partner: red or pink is usual.
  • At work: avoid hearts with clients unless you know them well. A thumbs up is safer.
  • In condolence messages: choose a quiet heart such as white, or no emoji at all.

Pairing hearts with words

A heart is warmest next to a few real words: "Thank you for coming ❤️" lands better than a row of hearts alone. Use one heart per message for most people. A close friend may enjoy several; a client may find even one too much.

When the colour matters

Colours often follow a team, a school, a flag or a festival: green for a sports side, saffron-like orange for a festival, white for a quiet moment. If you want everyone to read the same thing, pick red for love and yellow for friendship, and explain a surprising choice in words.
