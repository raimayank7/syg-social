Emoji people misread

Emoji that mean different things to different people: laughing and crying faces, the skull, the upside-down face, the thumbs up and more, with ways to avoid mix-ups.

Emoji are shared symbols with no dictionary. A few of them are used in very different ways by different age groups and communities, and that causes real misunderstandings. Here are the most common mix-ups.

The crying and laughing faces

😭 can mean sadness or it can mean laughing so hard you cry. 😂 is the classic laugh, but some people think of it as old-fashioned and use others instead. If the topic is serious, add a word.

The skull

💀 is a skull, and among many young people it means "I am dying of laughter", not anything about death. To older readers, it may look alarming. Avoid it in family groups unless everyone understands.

The thumbs up

👍 means okay for most people. Some younger users read it as cold or dismissive, and in a few cultures it can be rude. In chat with a friend it can feel curt, so add a word like "sure".

The upside-down face

🙃 is meant to be playful or ironic, but it can look passive aggressive. Use it only with people who know your tone.

The smile

🙂 may read as pleasant or as cool and sarcastic. When you want warmth, 😊 is clearer.

Folded hands

🙏 is "please", "thank you" or a prayer. Some read it as a high five. In India it also means namaste. When tone matters, write the words.

The fire, the hundred and the clap

🔥 means "great" or "hot", 💯 means "fully right", 👏 can mean praise or, between words, emphasis or sarcasm. These are the slang of young users, so check how your reader uses them.

How to avoid mix-ups

  • Use the emoji that you would use with a new colleague, not with a close friend.
  • If something could be read two ways, add a word.
  • Ask: "Did that sound rude? I did not mean it so."
