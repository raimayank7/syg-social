Face emoji meanings: how people read them

Plain meanings for the most used face emoji, with the usual reading, a note on how it can be misread and when to use a word instead.

Faces carry the tone of a message. These are the most common ones, how people usually read them and how they can be misread. Apps draw them differently, so the same emoji may look a little different to the person you write to.

😀 😃Happy, friendly.A big grin after bad news looks odd.
😂Laughing a lot.Some younger people have moved to other emoji for laughter.
🙂A mild, polite smile.Can read as cool or sarcastic.
😊Warm, pleased.Safe in nearly every context.
😅Nervous laugh, "whoops".Often means relief or awkwardness, not joy.
🥲Smiling while on the edge of tears.Mixed feelings; add a word.
😍Love, delight.For things as well as people.
🥰Warmth and affection.May feel too intimate for a stranger.

🤔Thinking, doubt.Can sound as if you doubt the person.
😴Sleepy.Harmless.
🤗A hug.Friendly, sometimes a gentle thank-you.
🤩Starstruck, very excited.Strong; use for real excitement.

Using faces well

  • One face at the end of a sentence is enough.
  • When the news is serious, use words, not faces.
  • With people you do not know, choose warm and plain: 😊 or 🙂.

Three short examples

"Reached home, thanks for the lift 😊" is warm and clear. "Meeting moved to 4 😅" tells the reader you know it is inconvenient. "Not happy with the delay 😡" is harsher than the same words alone, so in a work message write the sentence without the face and keep your tone calm.

How age and community change things

Younger users often treat some faces as old-fashioned and prefer others. Older readers may take a face more literally. If you write to a mixed family group, choose the faces everyone knows: the plain smile, the big grin, the heart eyes and the sad face. Save the odd ones for friends who share your habits.
